# Marble Run (Basalt sample)

A physics showcase built only through the automation API (`build_scene.json`). Five marbles roll down a
course, one after another:

- **Ramps** — static boxes under parent entities that tilt them; invisible panes in front of and behind the
  course keep the marbles on the track (a marble run's clear acrylic).
- **Seesaw** — a hinge with ±10° limits whose pivot sits right of center, so it rests tipped left until a
  marble lands on its right side and tips it.
- **Checkpoint** — a trigger zone that bursts sparks and rings a chime where it stands (a spatial one-shot).
- **Spinner** — two blades welded with a fixed joint and turned by a hinge's velocity motor; its hub hums
  (a looping spatial `AudioSource`).
- **Gate** — a slider whose position motor lifts it open and lowers it shut on a cycle.
- **Goal** — a trigger in the bucket that scores the marble, bursts confetti, chimes and removes it.
- **Kill floor** — a trigger under everything that counts lost marbles.
- **HUD** — UI components: the score, a progress bar (a slider nobody can drag), a Drop button, a spinner
  speed slider and a checkbox that holds the gate shut.

Marbles never sleep (`RigidBody.AllowSleeping = false`): a marble that turns around slowly on a ramp, or waits
behind the gate, would otherwise freeze where it stopped. Each one clacks through its own spatial
`AudioSource` when it hits something hard enough.

Scripts (`Assets/Scripts`):
- `Director.lua` releases the marbles (`Scene.Instantiate` of `Assets/Prefabs/Marble.bprefab`), cycles the
  gate, keeps the HUD current and handles the widgets' callbacks.
- `Sensor.lua` is shared by the three trigger zones; its properties pick the event it emits (`Events.Emit`),
  the burst size, the chime volume and whether it removes the marble. The Director keeps score from those
  events, so the sensors need not know about it.
- `Marble.lua` plays the clack.

The sounds are generated by `scripts/generate_sample_assets.py`; the font is Karla (SIL Open Font License,
`Assets/Fonts/Karla-OFL.txt`).

Play: `build/bin/BasaltRuntime --project Samples/MarbleRun`.
Rebuild the scene: `build/bin/basalt --project Samples/MarbleRun batch Samples/MarbleRun/build_scene.json`.

## The gameplay test

`test_game.json` (run by ctest as `SampleMarbleRun`) reads like the game's specification, written with the
automation API's assertions rather than Lua:

- `assert.entity_exists` / `assert.component` check the course before play (the goal is a trigger, the
  spinner's motor, the gate's slider, the hum is spatial) and the HUD after it (`Scored 5 / 5   Lost 0   Done!`).
- `input.mouse` clicks the Drop button, drags the speed slider and toggles the gate checkbox at their canvas
  positions (headless, mouse positions are canvas units); `assert.component` checks what they changed.
- `events.poll` with `expect` checks the contacts that matter: a marble landed on the seesaw, entered the
  checkpoint and the goal. Patterns name a contact's bodies in either order. A second subscription collects
  log lines, so the `MarbleRun: scored N` lines can be matched too.
- `scene.diff` against a snapshot taken before play shows the spinner turned (with `expectError`), and that
  stopping restored the edit scene exactly.
- One `lua.exec` covers what no assertion reaches: the sensors' counters (how many particles the bursts
  emitted) and that the hum is still playing.
