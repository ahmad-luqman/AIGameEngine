# Marble Run (Basalt sample)

A physics showcase built only through the automation API (`build_scene.json`). Five marbles roll down a
course, one after another:

- **Ramps** — static boxes under parent entities that tilt them; invisible panes in front of and behind the
  course keep the marbles on the track (a marble run's clear acrylic).
- **Seesaw** — a hinge with ±10° limits whose pivot sits right of center, so it rests tipped left until a
  marble lands on its right side and tips it.
- **Checkpoint** — a trigger zone that bursts sparks and rings a chime where it stands (a spatial one-shot).
- **Spinner** — two blades welded with a fixed joint and turned by a hinge's velocity motor; its hub hums
  (a looping spatial `AudioSource`).
- **Gate** — a slider whose position motor lifts it open and lowers it shut on a cycle.
- **Goal** — a trigger in the bucket that scores the marble, bursts confetti, chimes and removes it.
- **Kill floor** — a trigger under everything that counts lost marbles.
- **HUD** — UI components: the score, a progress bar (a slider nobody can drag), a Drop button, a spinner
  speed slider and a checkbox that holds the gate shut.

Marbles never sleep (`RigidBody.AllowSleeping = false`): a marble that slowly turns around on a ramp can come
to rest for the half second it takes to fall asleep, and then stays frozen there until something hits it. Each
one clacks through its own spatial `AudioSource` when it hits something hard enough.

Scripts (`Assets/Scripts`):
- `Director.lua` releases the marbles (`Scene.Instantiate` of `Assets/Prefabs/Marble.bprefab`), cycles the
  gate, keeps the HUD current and handles the widgets' callbacks.
- `Sensor.lua` is shared by the three trigger zones; its properties pick the event it emits (`Events.Emit`; the
  checkpoint emits none), the burst size, the chime volume and whether it removes the marble. The Director keeps
  score from those events, so the sensors need not know about it.
- `Marble.lua` plays the clack.

The sounds are generated by `scripts/generate_sample_assets.py`; the font is Karla (SIL Open Font License,
`Assets/Fonts/Karla-OFL.txt`).

Play: `build/bin/BasaltRuntime --project Samples/MarbleRun`.
Rebuild the scene: `build/bin/basalt --project Samples/MarbleRun batch Samples/MarbleRun/build_scene.json`.

## The gameplay test

`test_game.json` (run by ctest as `SampleMarbleRun`) reads like the game's specification, written with the
automation API's assertions rather than Lua:

- `assert.entity_exists` / `assert.component` check the course before play (the goal is a trigger, the
  spinner's motor, the gate's slider, the hum is spatial), a released marble (dynamic, never sleeps) and the HUD
  (`Scored 0 / 5   Lost 0` once the Director runs, `Scored 5 / 5   Lost 0   Done!` at the end).
- `input.mouse` clicks the Drop button, the middle of the speed slider and the gate checkbox. Headless, the
  canvas is 1920×1080 whatever the project's window size and mouse positions are canvas units (`ui.layout`
  lists the rects); `assert.component` checks what each click changed.
- The gate is held shut for about 16.5 seconds, so the marbles queue at rest behind it. The hold is checked during
  the gate cycle's open phase (the cycle is 1 s open, 1.5 s closed, so the step counts are tied to `GateOpenTime`
  and `GateClosedTime`; a `lua.exec` confirms the phase, so a timing change fails loudly instead of passing for the
  wrong reason) through the joint's motor target and the gate's position, and a `lua.exec` checks that no queued
  marble fell asleep (`e:IsSleeping()`). After the release, the gate is checked open and raised.
- `events.poll` with `expect` checks the contacts that matter: a marble landed on the seesaw, entered the
  checkpoint (twice: each pattern needs an event of its own), rolled on the last ramp and hit the gate, and
  later entered the goal. Patterns name a contact's bodies in either order. A second subscription collects log
  lines, so the `MarbleRun: scored 5` line is matched too.
- `scene.diff` against a snapshot taken before play shows the spinner turned (with `expectError`), and that
  stopping restored the edit scene exactly.
- A few `lua.exec` steps cover what no assertion reaches: the seesaw tipped (the Director records its lowest
  angle), the sensors' counters (how many particles the bursts emitted), that the hum is still playing, and that a
  removed marble entering the goal again (or the kill floor) in the same frame counts once.
